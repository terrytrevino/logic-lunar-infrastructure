INDEPENDENT RESEARCH CONCEPT · PRELIMINARY

Direct Solar-Thermal & Daylighting Architecture

Fixed-base, sun-tracking optical concentrators for lunar facilities

Charcoal gray · unvalidated concept

Research identity and boundaries

Concept development: D. Terry Trevino, with ChatGPT-assisted technical exploration. This is an independent research direction, not a LOGIC team deliverable, endorsed LOGIC architecture, or funded program. LOGIC may later benefit from findings, subject to separate agreement and attribution. Publication here is for contextual technical discussion and does not transfer project ownership.

Physical concept

Site-mounted tracking reflective dishes concentrate sunlight at a controlled receiver. Candidate locations: habitat vicinity, regolith processing plant, and energy farm. Candidate uses: enclosed water-loop heating, regolith processing/sintering, thermal storage charging, and separately conditioned daylighting. A dish focal point is local; long-distance light routing would require additional optics.

Preliminary calculation

At solar irradiance 1,361 W/m², a 2 m diameter circular aperture intercepts π × (1 m)² × 1,361 ≈ 4.28 kW at normal incidence. A hypothetical 75% end-to-end delivery factor yields 3.21 kW at the receiver. Neither the efficiency nor the usable heat has been demonstrated for this system.

Feasibility questions

Low solar elevation and horizon blockage at the lunar south pole; optical tracking and receiver geometry; dust fouling; thermal gradients and regolith conductivity; storage insulation and heat recovery; receiver interchangeability; crew-safe illumination; mass and deployment constraints; comparison against photovoltaics plus resistive heating; prior art and NIAC novelty.

First analyses

1. Model local solar visibility and aperture orientation. 2. Build collector/receiver loss budget. 3. Evaluate regolith thermal diffusivity, storage mass and discharge power. 4. Compare direct thermal processing with electrical alternatives. 5. Evaluate separately the photometric and safety requirements of habitat daylighting.
